Project Overview

What I Built

An end-to-end analytics pipeline that ingests raw job postings from multiple sources, engineers structured intelligence through NLP, stores results in PostgreSQL, and produces analytical outputs for Power BI and Python visualization.

⚙

Multi-source ingestion, validation, deduplication, and cleaning of 8,256 job postings from Adzuna UK and CHISEL/LUMS Pakistan.

🔍

NLP & Skill Extraction

Lexicon-based skill extraction with contextual disambiguation, reducing false positives by 75% through pattern analysis and validation.

📊

Transparency

Limitations

Analytical credibility requires acknowledging what the data cannot tell us.

  • UK and Pakistan datasets are not temporally equivalent. UK data is recent (2023–2026); Pakistan data is historical (2019–2021). Direct comparison reflects dataset period, not necessarily current market conditions.
  • Job postings represent employer demand, not the complete labor market. Self-employed, internal hires, and word-of-mouth placements are underrepresented.
  • The CHISEL/LUMS Pakistan dataset classifies 99% of roles as “other” due to its original taxonomy, limiting role-specific analysis for Pakistan.
  • Salary analysis is UK-only. The Pakistan CHISEL dataset does not contain comparable salary information. A small Rozee.pk sample (16 records) is insufficient for comparison.
  • Skill extraction is limited to 78 canonical skills. Domain-specific or emerging skills not in the lexicon are not captured.
  • London concentration (76%) reflects Adzuna’s UK coverage, not necessarily the entire UK data labor market.
  • Pakistan Data Analyst career intelligence is based on 21 postings. Sample sizes for some breakdowns are small and should be interpreted cautiously.
  • 100% of Pakistan postings are classified as on-site, reflecting the CHISEL dataset’s collection methodology rather than the actual Pakistan work-mode distribution.
Key Findings

What the Data Shows

Evidence-based findings from the analytical layer. Each finding is supported by the validated Phase 5 outputs.

Two Markets

UK and PK Skill Profiles Are Fundamentally Different

UK emphasizes reporting, ML, and BI tools. Pakistan emphasizes PHP, communication, and web technologies. Chi-square test confirms significant difference (p ≈ 0).

UK Market

UK Data Roles Are Well-Structured

46.5% of UK postings are classified as Data Analyst, Data Scientist, or Analytics Engineer. The remaining 48% are “other” roles that require data-adjacent skills.

Pakistan Market

Pakistan Has a Web-Development-Heavy Tech Market

PHP, JavaScript, HTML, CSS, C#, and Java dominate Pakistan’s skill profile, reflecting a software-development-heavy job market during the CHISEL collection period (2019–2021).

Geography

Different Geographic Concentration Patterns

UK: 76.3% London-dominated. Pakistan: three-city distribution (Lahore 31.8%, Islamabad 28.4%, Karachi 25.3%).

Seniority

Pakistan Skews Heavily Junior

75.4% of Pakistan postings are junior-level vs 49.8% in UK. Only 1.3% of Pakistan postings are senior vs 15.9% in UK.

Career

SQL and Python Are Universal Requirements

SQL and Python appear across all three target roles in the UK. In Pakistan, Excel and SQL lead the Data Analyst profile. Python appears in both markets for Data Scientist roles.
